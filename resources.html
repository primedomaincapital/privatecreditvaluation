Where private credit gets its number.

Last checked: November 6, 2026

A private loan has no market price, so the number on it has to be built — from regulation, from transaction data, from audit practice, and from the market's own research. The links below cover all four sides: the regulators who now test the marks, the industry research that sizes the market, the valuation firms that build the benchmarks, and the reporting that tracks what happens when the marks are challenged.

Regulators and official sources

SEC — Statement on Fair Value Measurement and Disclosure Considerations for Private Assets

Joint staff statement of September 28, 2026 on private credit valuation, calibration, the NAV practical expedient, and non-accrual and payment-in-kind disclosure.

Federal Reserve — Financial Stability Report, May 2026, Funding Risks

The Fed's May 2026 report, including its private credit box: about $1.4 trillion of U.S. private credit loans and the redemption pressure at semi-liquid vehicles.

Financial Stability Board — Report on Vulnerabilities in Private Credit

The FSB's first dedicated report on private credit vulnerabilities, May 6, 2026: a $1.5–2 trillion market, redemption pressures, hidden leverage, and bank interconnectedness.

Morgan Stanley's outlook for the asset class, including return and risk considerations and the $5 trillion projection for 2029.

PitchBook — Private credit AUM could hit $4.5 trillion by 2030

PitchBook's account of BlackRock's March 2025 forecast, including the AI-infrastructure buildout behind the projection.

Arcesium — Behind the Private Credit Surge

Overview of the asset class's growth since 2009, with S&P Global data on private debt's 77 percent share of 2024 leveraged buyout financing.
