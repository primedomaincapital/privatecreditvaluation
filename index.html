The thesis

A name that states the number the market has to trust.

A public bond has a price because someone traded it yesterday. A private loan has no such price. Its value is a judgment — built from spreads, comparable deals, borrower financials, and a model — and that judgment now sits at the center of a multi-trillion-dollar market.

That is why this name works as a business identity rather than a description. A fund manager reading it knows the subject is the mark on the portfolio. An auditor reading it knows the subject is the evidence behind the mark. An investor reading it knows the subject is whether the NAV they are buying into is real.

The name is exact, pronounceable, and hard to mistake for anything else. It works on a valuation firm's letterhead, on a fund administrator's NAV platform, on a pricing-benchmark product, and in front of a regulator who wants to know, in one glance, that this is a valuation business first — not a lender, not a fund, not a broker.

Specific, and broad enough to travel. A strong valuation brand must cover three linked jobs at once: the independent opinion on a portfolio, the recurring NAV and reporting work that funds outsource, and the benchmarks and data that let a manager defend a mark. This name covers valuation advisory, fund administration, and pricing data without narrowing to any single one — and without vagueness that says nothing at all.
